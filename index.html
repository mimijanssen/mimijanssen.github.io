I am a PhD candidate in the Psychological and Brain Sciences department at Dartmouth College working with Dr. Matthijs van der Meer. I hold a B.S. in Bioengineering and B.A. in Economics from the University of Maryland. Prior to Dartmouth College, I worked as a post-baccalaureate fellow under the mentorship of Dr. Bruno Averbeck in the Section on Learning and Decision Making at the National Institute of Mental Health.

My PhD research, which is supported by an NSF grfp, seeks to test neurophysiological predictions from a recent computational theory that ties together replay of past experiences with value learning via teaching signals. More broadly, I'm interested how affective past experiences shape learning and memory, within the mesocorticolimbic system, using computational and systems neuroscience approaches.

Research Questions

Are hippocampal replay events coupled with ventral striatal teaching signals?

Motivated by a computational theory where prioritized replay of past experiences generates teaching signals to drive learning, my first Ph.D. project sought to test these predictions within the brain. As an initial step, I investigated the relationship between putative hippocampal replay events, sharp-wave ripples (SWRs), and ventral striatal dopamine teaching signals. Using microdrive recordings and fiber photometry, I found that there is a transient increase in dopamine signals following SWRs, suggesting that replay events and teaching signals are linked together. My ongoing Ph.D. work is further testing a key prediction of this computational framework: whether replay content determines the sign of the teaching signal.

What are the properties of hippocampal ensemble encoding and reactivation of odor stimuli?

Hippocampal encoding and subsequent reactivation have been mostly studied within the contiguous spatial domain. To extend this work to discrete stimuli that can be more precisely controlled, we are using a sequential odor task. By performing high-density recordings using Neuropixel probes within the hippocampus, we aim to characterize odor-specific hippocampal activity.

Is there a bias in the encoding and reactivation of valenced information across the dorsoventral hippocampal axis?

Given that the ventral hippocampus has strong anatomical connections with the ventral striatum (which is associated with value processing) and the amygdala (which is associated with emotional processing), and that the dorsal and ventral hippocampus are functionally and anatomically dissociated, we are investigating whether the ventral hippocampus is more involved in the the encoding and reactivation of valenced odor stimuli and outcomes.

Are rats asymmetric value-learners?

In a collaboration with Dr. Catherine Holland, a postdoctoral researcher at Yale University, and Dr. Eléonore Duvelle, a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Glasgow, we are investigating whether rats learn values non-linearly using a dynamic multi-armed bandit task.
